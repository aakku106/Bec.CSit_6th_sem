## Study Priority Summary

1. **Master first:** Brief Correspondence — letters/emails/memos ([[TW/Syllabus#Unit 4. Brief Correspondence (4 Hrs.)\|Unit 4]], asked in every single paper), Employment Communication — resumes & cover letters ([[TW/Syllabus#Unit 8. Employment Communication (5 Hrs.)\|Unit 8]]), core definitions of Technical Writing ([[TW/Syllabus#Unit 1. What Is Technical Writing (3 Hrs.)\|Unit 1]]), and Information Reports — press releases, mechanism/process description, progress reports ([[TW/Syllabus#Unit 7. Information Reports (5 Hrs.)\|Unit 7]]). These four alone appear in almost every year's paper.
2. **Then cover:** Document Design & Graphics ([[TW/Syllabus#Unit 5. Document Design and Graphics (4 Hrs.)\|Unit 5]]), Writing for the Web ([[TW/Syllabus#Unit 6. Writing for the Web (4 Hrs.)\|Unit 6]]), the Writing Process incl. collaborative writing & peer review ([[TW/Syllabus#Unit 3. Writing Process (4 Hrs.)\|Unit 3]]), Presentations/speeches ([[TW/Syllabus#Unit 9. Presentations (5 Hrs.)\|Unit 9]]), Proposals ([[TW/Syllabus#Unit 11. Proposals (3 Hrs.)\|Unit 11]]), Ethics ([[TW/Syllabus#Unit 12. Ethics in the Workplace (2 Hrs.)\|Unit 12]]), and Audience & Purpose ([[TW/Syllabus#Unit 2. Audience and Purpose (3 Hrs.)\|Unit 2]]).
3. **Last, if time remains:** Recommendation Reports ([[TW/Syllabus#Unit 10. Recommendation Reports (3 Hrs.)\|Unit 10]]), Gantt charts as a standalone skill, defining scattered key terms, and legacy grammar-style questions (sentence types) that no longer map cleanly onto the current syllabus.

> Note: Nearly every paper is scenario-based ("write a letter/email/memo/report for X situation") rather than pure theory — practicing full write-ups for different realistic IT/business scenarios is far more valuable than memorizing definitions alone.
